# The Ashenveil Chronicles — Chapter 4: The Night the Lock-Box Broke

## The Trap Door Fails

On the forty-third night of Maren's stay in Thornmere, the trapdoor above the iron lock-box failed. The middle wooden latch cracked — old rot that had been growing unseen for years. The trapdoor could no longer be sealed completely. A gap of three inches remained.

Three inches was enough. Iron resonance seeped upward through the gap into the night air.

Aldric discovered the failure at midnight during his patrol. He came immediately to Maren at the inn.

"The lock-box is open," he said. "The middle latch. Three inches of gap."

Maren did not hesitate. She dressed and went with him.

## Maren Uses the Salt Water Protocol

At the lock-box, they assessed the damage. The resonance was already leaking — they could feel it as a faint pressure behind the ears, which Maren had learned was how sensitive people experienced iron resonance.

"We have to seal it with something non-iron immediately," Maren said. "But the resonance trace is already out. We need to cancel it."

She sent Aldric for the salt water supplies from the village storehouse. He returned with two large ceramic vessels.

"Pour them," she said. "Directly over the trapdoor opening and along the ground in all directions for ten feet."

Aldric poured. The faint pressure behind their ears faded.

"It worked," he said.

"Temporarily," Maren said. "The resonance trail outside is cancelled. But the iron in the box is still resonating. We need to seal the gap before the Hollow orient on the box itself."

*She understood clearly: the salt water had cancelled the trail that had already escaped, but the open gap meant the iron inside was still broadcasting. The salt water had bought them time — perhaps twenty minutes — before the Hollow would locate the source directly rather than following a trail.*

They used three layers of oilcloth, a wooden wedge, and a river-stone pressed into the gap. It wasn't a proper seal, but it reduced the leak to almost nothing.

No Hollow came that night.

## The Formal Record

In the morning, the village elder — an old woman named Councillor Ystra — came to inspect the lock-box. She documented the incident formally, as was required by Thornmere law.

She noted, in the official ledger, that Maren Ashcroft had correctly applied the salt water protocol and improvised a seal under pressure. She wrote: *"Maren Ashcroft demonstrated complete competence in emergency iron management. Her application of salt water resonance cancellation was timely and correct."*

This was the first official recognition of Maren in the village records.

## The Binding's Consequence

After the lock-box incident, Aldric proposed that they formally extend their binding oath. He wanted Maren to take on the role of village warden — a permanent position, not a seasonal one.

Maren hesitated. She had not yet dissolved her current binding, and Thornmere's rules about overlapping oaths were strict: you could not make a new binding while an old one was still in effect. The current binding would expire naturally at the end of the Snow Moon — two weeks away.

"Wait two weeks," she said. "When the current oath expires, I'll consider the permanent one."

Aldric agreed.

## Fenwick Leaves

Fenwick left Thornmere at dawn on the forty-fifth day. He had everything he needed. Before he went, he pressed a folded letter into Maren's hand.

"I've written to the Academy about your work here," he said. "They will want to meet you again. On your terms this time — I've made that clear in the letter."

Maren tucked the letter into her coat without reading it.

Aldric watched Fenwick's cart disappear into the tree line. Then he turned to Maren.

"Do you trust him?" he said.

"No," she said. "But I trust that he was honest about his information."
