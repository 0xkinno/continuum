# The Ashenveil Chronicles — Chapter 2: Iron and Salt

## Three Rules, Not One

By the second week, Maren had learned that Thornmere had more than one law. The First Law — no iron after dark — she already knew. The second and third she learned from Aldric during a long afternoon mending the fence at the village's northern edge.

**The Second Law: A warding circle must be completed in a single motion. Any break in the chalk line renders it inert.**

Aldric had seen a traveller try to patch a broken ward once, going back over the gap with fresh chalk. The ward had failed that night. Two Hollow had reached the traveller's window. He hadn't made that mistake again.

**The Third Law: Salt water cleanses the resonance trail that iron leaves behind.** If iron was accidentally left uncovered, salt water poured over the ground where it had lain would cancel the resonance trace. A Hollow following the trail would lose the scent within forty feet.

Maren wrote both laws in her notebook. She now understood the complete picture: iron drew the Hollow; salt water erased iron's trail; and wards, if drawn correctly in one unbroken motion, could slow a Hollow for up to an hour.

## The Lock-Box

Aldric showed her the village's lock-box: a deep iron chest buried six feet underground in the village square, accessible only through a trapdoor sealed with three wooden latches. Every piece of iron in Thornmere was stored here at dusk. It had been this way for sixty years.

"What happens if someone doesn't store their iron in time?" Maren asked.

"If they pour salt water over where it lay, the Hollow won't track it here," Aldric said. "If they don't — the Hollow will come to wherever the resonance is strongest. Usually the bedroom."

"And if someone pours the salt water, then leaves even a single iron nail uncovered?"

Aldric looked at her steadily. "Then the salt water means nothing. The nail broadcasts louder than the erased trail. Salt water only works when there is no iron currently resonating."

Maren noted this carefully. *Salt water cancels a prior resonance trail. It cannot neutralise actively resonating iron.*

## The Binding Oath

That afternoon, Maren made a decision. She would stay in Thornmere for the winter. She made a formal binding with Aldric: she would stand secondary night watch twice a week in exchange for instruction in how the warding circles worked.

A binding oath between two people in Thornmere was a serious matter. Once sworn, it could only be dissolved if both parties agreed, or if the village elder witnessed a formal release. Aldric and Maren both knew this. They swore the oath in the village square, in front of the well, with old Bram Colwick watching.

From that moment, Maren was committed to the arrangement. She could not simply leave without formally dissolving the oath.

## Maren's Discovery

At the end of the chapter, while cataloguing her herbs in the cellar of the inn, Maren discovered something important: among the traveller's goods left behind in a storage chest, there was a small iron compass — old, dull, seemingly harmless.

But it was iron. She poured a full pitcher of salt water over the flagstones where it sat.

Then she wrapped it in oilcloth and buried it two feet deep in the kitchen garden before sunset. She did not tell Aldric. She told herself she would mention it in the morning.
