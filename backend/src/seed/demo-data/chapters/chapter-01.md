# The Ashenveil Chronicles — Chapter 1: The Warding Circle

## The Village of Thornmere

The village of Thornmere sat at the edge of the Ashenveil Forest, a place where the trees grew so close together that full daylight rarely reached the ground. The people there had one law above all others, spoken at every child's naming ceremony and carved into the lintel above the village gate:

**All iron must be buried before nightfall. A blade left uncovered after dark will draw the Hollow.**

It was a rule as old as the village itself. Iron called to the Hollow — those pale, eyeless things that drifted through the forest at night. Every blacksmith, every traveller, every soldier who passed through Thornmere learned this immediately. Cover your iron or bury it. This was the First Law, and it had never been broken without consequence.

## Maren's Arrival

Maren Ashcroft arrived in Thornmere on the third day of the Harvest Moon, carrying nothing but a satchel of herbs and a bone-handled knife at her belt. She was twenty-four years old, dark-haired, and deliberately plain in her dress — the grey wool of a hedge-witch, not a court mage. The bone handle mattered: no iron.

She knocked at the inn door just as the sun touched the treeline. The innkeeper, old Bram Colwick, noticed the knife immediately.

"Bone-handled," he said. "Good. You know the First Law."

"I was told in the last town," Maren said. "No iron after dark."

She had not been told the full reason — only the rule itself. She did not yet know what the Hollow were, or why iron drew them.

## Aldric and the Night Watch

Aldric Voss had patrolled the perimeter of Thornmere every night for eleven years. He was thirty-one, broad-shouldered, with a scar that ran from his left ear to his chin — a reminder of his first encounter with a Hollow when he was seventeen. He carried no iron on his person during night watch; instead, he used a carved oakwood staff and a sling made of river-leather.

That night, he found Maren sitting on the inn steps watching the tree line.

"You shouldn't be outside after dark," he said.

"I'm not afraid of the dark," she replied.

"It's not the dark you should be afraid of." He sat beside her. "How much did they tell you in the last town?"

"Just the rule," she said. "No iron. Something comes."

He told her about the Hollow: creatures of hollow sound and stolen breath, drawn by the vibration that iron made in the night air. Cold iron resonated at a frequency the Hollow could sense from half a mile. Wood, bone, stone — nothing. But a single uncovered blade could bring three or four of them to a village.

Maren listened carefully. She committed everything to memory. *The Hollow are drawn by the resonance of cold iron. Wood, bone, and stone do not attract them.*

This was the first night. She would not forget.

## The Warding Circle

Before she slept, Maren drew a small warding circle in the earth outside her window using powdered chalk and pressed river-stone. It was the first ward she had ever made in a real emergency, and she was careful to make it correctly — no iron implements, only wood and chalk and intention.

Aldric watched from the path.

"That will help," he said.

"Only a little," she said. "The ward slows them. It doesn't stop them."

"I know," he said. "Nothing stops them. That's why we bury the iron."
